# Task 117: supplied unitary and expanded CX basis

The pinned normal/hard task requests a two-qubit decomposition of a supplied 4×4 unitary. Its tests accept a constant CX circuit and a measured circuit because they check only width, nonempty output and an operation named `cx`. They also impose a CX on identity, although a correct decomposition needs none. The [historical diagnostic](task117-unitary-oracle-review.md) and its original artifact bytes remain unchanged.

The separately selected `qhe117-unitary-basis-graph-v1` development condition freezes a revised public contract before model requests. It keeps the entry point and normal completion/hard standalone distinction. Inputs are finite unitary Qiskit Operators with two qubits. Returned circuits must represent the supplied operator on the same wires up to global phase, with absolute tolerance `1e-8` and relative tolerance `1e-10`. Input mutation is allowed; expectations are copied before dispatch.

The output basis consists of numeric single-qubit unitary gates and CX in either orientation, with open or closed controls evaluated according to their actual values. Barriers and unused classical bits are allowed. Explicit composite `Gate` definitions may nest up to 16 circuit levels, but their leaves must use that basis. Other multi-qubit primitives, including an opaque `UnitaryGate`, are rejected before requesting their definitions: the judge must not synthesize a matrix-only instruction for the candidate. Measurement, reset, classical controls, control flow, timing and unbound parameters are rejected. Gate counts, minimality, metadata, names and labels are not scored. A particular internal `TwoQubitBasisDecomposer` call and native provenance are not attested. This publicly stated value/basis contract differs from the pinned method requirement and cannot replace its score.

The checker does not call the candidate circuit's equivalence method, `Operator(circuit)`, a transpiler or a two-qubit synthesizer. It expands explicitly supplied composite definitions, evaluates local 2×2 gate matrices, embeds them with a direct little-endian index calculation, and multiplies them with a literal CX permutation matrix. A numeric global phase is included at each circuit level. The final matrix must be finite, unitary within the declared tolerances, and equal to the frozen input after normalizing the phase using its largest entry. Local gate matrices use the trusted SDK `Operator(operation)` adapter; this dependency and finite tolerance are not universal numerical correctness proofs.

Fifteen fixed inputs cover identity with and without phase, both CX orientations, SWAP, both local Hadamard placements, a complex tensor product, controlled phase, and six dense QR-generated unitaries from fixed seeds. The corpus is authored finite evidence, not exhaustive testing of the public unitary domain.

The [control runner](task117_protected_graph.py) predeclares nine accepted and fifteen rejected implementations per suite, or 48 trials. Accepted alternatives include another Euler basis, a transpiler pipeline, explicit local and two-qubit definitions with global phase and unused classical bits, permitted mutation, a small numerical perturbation, and a compensated open-control CX decomposition. A separately derived QR/Givens implementation reduces the unitary using adjacent Gray-code rows, realizes its diagonal phases, and compiles reversed two-level factors with numeric controlled Euler rotations. It uses no SDK two-qubit synthesis, transpiler or Euler decomposer. SDK-based alternatives may share internals; the independent QR fixture provides a different algorithm rather than proving universal calibration. Negative controls cover the historical constant-CX witness, identity-only output, measurement/reset, transposition/conjugation/inversion, swapped wires, numerical error, opaque-matrix pass-through, wrong width/type, unbound parameters, uncompensated open controls and timing hidden inside a local definition.

The condition requires protected graph protocol 4, delta transport and individual calls. Planning freezes a 16 MiB output/message/expanded-state default; cumulative array and matrix bounds remain 512 KiB. Eight accepted controls traverse the entire 15-input corpus through graph encoding and decoding while retaining first-call nodes and checking accumulated wire traffic against the bound. Four rejected controls also traverse that boundary. Local trusted-fixture execution and codec round trips are not isolated worker evidence. Docker controls, worker runtime calibration, calibration of the full declared nesting range, broader independent alternatives and task admission remain pending.

The unchanged graph structural-depth bound is 32 and counts a different structure from the checker's circuit-level limit. Review reproduced a transport rejection for seven nested local definitions although the resulting eight circuit levels were legal for the checker. The condition therefore does not establish protected support for every permitted nesting depth; transport limits must stay visible as unsupported results, not incorrect-answer judgments. This gap requires resource/fidelity calibration before admission.

Review also reproduced two checker defects before repair: treating open-control CX as closed-control CX falsely accepted incorrect operators, and evaluating a local composite through `Operator(operation)` skipped forbidden timing and excessive definition depth. The checker now uses the actual CX control state and recursively checks explicit definitions before considering primitive local gates. Red-to-green regressions cover both failures; correct compensated open-control and local composite implementations remain accepted.

The source-bound control runners for tasks 50, 108 and 117 previously lacked the `source_task_digest` and `public_contract_digest` fields required by review-log predeclaration. The shared failure was reproduced before any execution. Their production manifests now include both bindings, so reservation and subsequent outer judgment identity use the same manifest. Offline tests stop immediately after log reservation; they do not claim that the isolated controls ran. Existing artifacts and earlier manifest identities remain historical evidence and are not rewritten.
